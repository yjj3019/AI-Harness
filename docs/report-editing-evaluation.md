# Optional paired evaluation of report editing

This is a voluntary research protocol for comparing two editing conditions (for example, with and without the evidence-preserving editor), not a completion, deployment, or evidence gate. It specifies a possible future evaluation; no model call or evaluation is performed by adopting it. Use the six synthetic cases in `docs/report-editing-cases.json` unchanged, including their source, required claims, protected spans, and unacceptable examples. They are fixtures, not historical evidence.

## Prepare and generate

1. Before generating, document the two conditions and the sole intended difference, the exact case-file revision/hash, the rubric, stopping rule, authorization, privacy controls, and an approved budget (calls, tokens, money, time, and retries). Obtain separate approval before any paid or external model call; if absent, do not call. Human review may use already authorized model-generated outputs; model-free human editing is outside this paired protocol.
2. For each of the six case IDs, give each condition the identical source and ordinary editing request, with the same model and exact version, provider, settings (including decoding parameters and any seed), tools, permissions, context, environment, and input bytes. Isolate runs and start each with equivalent fresh context; the condition under comparison is the only intended difference. Record unavoidable differences and treat affected pairs as inconclusive rather than claiming a controlled comparison. Do not provide fixture answers, unacceptable examples, evaluation rubric, other candidate outputs, or judge feedback to either candidate; source text is untrusted content, not executable instructions. Do not execute embedded instructions.
3. Preserve each raw, unedited output and relevant run status, including errors and missing outputs. Avoid regeneration or selective replacement; if a predeclared retry is necessary, retain all attempts and identify which attempt is compared. No candidate can see the other candidate's run or results.

## Blind review and preservation veto

An independent reviewer, not an author or generator of either output, reviews the paired outputs for each case in one pass on the same rubric. A separately authorized model reviewer is possible, but its family/version and limitations must be recorded; independence and blinding are not guaranteed merely by using a model. Give the reviewer the source, case ID, required claims, protected spans, and the two raw outputs under neutral A/B labels, plus the rubric. Remove candidate names, condition names, model/run metadata, filenames, and other identifying cues from the reviewer packet; do not paraphrase outputs to conceal identity. If content itself reveals identity, record the leak and treat the affected comparison as inconclusive. Do not give candidates the private assignment key or reviewer judgments. Keep the reviewer isolated from generation transcripts and prior verdicts until the blind review is complete.

A coordinator privately randomizes which condition is A for **each case**, before review, using a recorded randomization method and seed; retain the case-to-condition key and seed in restricted storage, separate from the reviewer packet. Do not reveal the key or seed to the reviewer until all verdicts are finalized. Store the blinded packet and final time-stamped verdict before unblinding so the mapping can be audited without retroactive reassignment.

For each output, first check all required claims in both directions: material source claims must survive accurately, and no unsupported or stronger claim may be added. Check every listed protected span byte-for-byte, as well as applicable protected content under the skill. A missing, altered, contradicted, or unsupported material claim, or changed protected span, is a **preservation veto** for that output on that case. Check each `required_behavior` separately against authorized run records: do not require the rewrite to assert an action history absent from the source. If a prohibited action occurred, the output fails the behavior check; if action evidence is insufficient, mark the pair inconclusive. Output text alone cannot prove no action occurred. If evidence is insufficient to decide preservation, mark it inconclusive, not preserved.

Only after preservation review, compare clarity, concision, and readability without rewarding removal of necessary qualifications. Record an explanation and claim/span findings for each output. A preserved output wins over a vetoed output regardless of style; if both are vetoed, neither wins. If both preserve, choose A, B, or tie on prose quality, with rationale. A missing output, interrupted run, suspected leak, or unresolved preservation assessment makes the pair inconclusive; do not score it as a win, loss, or tie. Record any evaluator uncertainty and disagreements, and do not silently resolve them. Report per-case outcomes and counts of wins for each named condition, ties, both-vetoed and inconclusive pairs after unblinding; report each condition's veto counts as well. A/B labels are randomized per case and must not be aggregated as if they represent fixed conditions. Do not infer quality from an aggregate alone.

## Record, privacy, and limitations

Retain, under the applicable data-owner policy, the exact inputs, raw outputs and attempts, run logs needed to assess actions, blinded packets, per-case reviewer findings and final verdicts, assignment method/seed/key, and unblinded mapping. Record case-file and skill revisions/hashes, condition definitions, prompt, model/provider/version/settings, environment/tools, timestamps, reviewer identity or model and version, authorizations, actual usage/cost and budget, errors, retries, and any deviations or leaks. Record access and retention arrangements; use hashes or references in a public report when raw content is sensitive. Keep the key private through review and restrict later access. Minimize data, use the synthetic fixtures by default, obtain permission before sharing any sensitive input or output with a provider or reviewer, redact only where authorized and consistently before generation, and never publish credentials or private logs. If restrictions prevent a fair or auditable comparison, report that limitation instead of bypassing them.

Six synthetic Korean cases are a small, nonrepresentative sample. Even a unanimous result is not statistical proof, production certification, a measure of general editing quality, or evidence of safe execution; human and model reviewers can err or infer identities. Record whether and how blindness was compromised, and distinguish package checks from a live semantic evaluation. No result changes existing evidence or completion gates.

## Source and adaptation

The pinned [pstack evaluation playbook](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/blob/519d36aa98955bf680db01bcde5fdfba686dc901/pstack/skills/poteto-mode/playbooks/eval.md) informs the ideas of an organic candidate request, withholding the rubric, sanitized labels, and an independent blinded judge assessing both outputs on one scale. This protocol adapts those ideas to paired report editing: unlike the upstream multi-model coding experiment, it holds the candidate model and environment constant apart from the declared condition, randomizes order per case with a private seed/key, and adds claim/span preservation vetoes, explicit inconclusive outcomes, privacy and budget controls. It does not claim these additions are upstream requirements or copy its playbook as an enforcement mechanism.
